## 🟦 Commerce

> Market-facing domains that drive customer acquisition, retention, and value creation through positioning, pricing, and customer experience.

### `C1` · Demand

*Commerce × Availability*

> Strategic exposure to market needs and purchasing intent

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Addressable market size and revenue potential
- Identified market segments and customer profiles
- Documented customer needs and pain points
- Current purchasing intent signals (inquiries, leads, interest)

### `C2` · Competitiveness

*Commerce × Adaptability*

> Ability to reconfigure market position and value proposition to remain relevant

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Market share and competitive position
- Win/loss rate against competitors
- Speed of value proposition adjustment
- Market positioning flexibility

### `C3` · Desirability

*Commerce × Effectiveness*

> How well the offer aligns with what customers actually want

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Customer satisfaction scores (CSAT, NPS)
- Product-market fit indicators
- Willingness to pay and pricing power
- Feature adoption and usage rates

### `C4` · Conversion

*Commerce × Efficiency*

> Ratio of market attention successfully turned into paying customers

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Lead-to-customer conversion rate
- Cost per acquisition (CAC)
- Revenue per lead and per channel
- Average transaction value

### `C5` · Loyalty

*Commerce × Sustainability*

> Ability to maintain long-term, repeat engagement and preference from customers

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Customer retention rate
- Repeat purchase frequency
- Recurring revenue and revenue retention (net/gross)
- Customer lifetime value (CLV)

## 🟨 Operations

> Internal execution domains that transform inputs into outputs through efficient processes, resource utilization, and service delivery.

### `O1` · Capacity

*Operations × Availability*

> Total usable operational resources ready to be activated

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Production capacity and throughput limits
- Available equipment and infrastructure
- Current inventory and material stock levels
- Facility space and physical resources

### `O2` · Responsiveness

*Operations × Adaptability*

> Speed and flexibility with which operations react to new constraints or opportunities

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Process reconfiguration speed
- Demand surge response capability
- Supply chain flexibility
- Resource reallocation agility

### `O3` · Performance

*Operations × Effectiveness*

> Reliability and consistency in meeting operational output targets

**Examples of what it covers:**

- On-time delivery rate
- Quality assurance metrics
- First-pass yield and rework rate
- Service level agreement (SLA) compliance

### `O4` · Optimization

*Operations × Efficiency*

> How well inputs (time, materials, energy) are minimized per output unit

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Unit cost of production
- Resource utilization rates
- Waste and defect rates
- Cycle time and throughput metrics

### `O5` · Continuity

*Operations × Sustainability*

> Ability to sustain operational functioning over time despite disruption

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Business continuity plan effectiveness
- Redundancy and backup systems
- Mean time between failures (MTBF)
- Mean time to recovery (MTTR)

## 🟩 People

> Human capital domains encompassing talent acquisition, development, retention, and organizational culture that enables all other business functions.

### `P1` · Workforce

*People × Availability*

> Access to the human resources needed to operate and grow

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Current headcount and role coverage
- Talent pipeline and candidate pool
- Critical skill availability
- Critical role fill rate and bench strength

### `P2` · Mobility

*People × Adaptability*

> Capacity to shift, reskill, or redeploy talent to match evolving needs

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Internal mobility and promotion rates
- Cross-functional capability
- Reskilling and upskilling programs
- Role flexibility and job rotation

### `P3` · Engagement

*People × Effectiveness*

> Level of emotional and cognitive commitment from employees to their work

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Employee engagement scores
- Discretionary effort indicators
- Performance above baseline
- Absenteeism and attendance rates

### `P4` · Utilization

*People × Efficiency*

> Degree to which employee time and skills are applied to productive work

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Labor productivity metrics
- Time allocation to value-adding activities
- Billable vs. non-billable hours
- Skill-to-task match rate

### `P5` · Belonging

*People × Sustainability*

> Durable sense of fit, purpose, and psychological safety in the workplace

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Employee retention rate
- Voluntary turnover reasons
- Diversity, equity, and inclusion (DEI) metrics
- Inclusion and psychological safety metrics

## 🟧 Finance

> Financial management domains that ensure sustainable resource allocation, risk management, and value creation through strategic financial decisions.

### `F1` · Cash

*Finance × Availability*

> Immediate liquidity and financial room to maneuver

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Cash on hand and available balances
- Days cash on hand
- Operating cash flow runway
- Quick ratio and current ratio

### `F2` · Liquidity

*Finance × Adaptability*

> Ability to shift financial resources or structure in response to change

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Asset liquidity and conversion speed
- Financial restructuring capability
- Capital reallocation flexibility
- Debt refinancing options

### `F3` · ROI

*Finance × Effectiveness*

> Return generated from a financial input over time

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Return on investment (ROI)
- Return on assets (ROA)
- Return on equity (ROE)
- Revenue per dollar invested

### `F4` · Profitability

*Finance × Efficiency*

> Ratio of earned income to costs, reflecting resource yield

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Revenue growth rate
- Gross and net profit margins
- EBITDA and EBITDA margin
- Operating expense ratio

### `F5` · Solvency

*Finance × Sustainability*

> Capacity to meet obligations and remain financially viable over the long term

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Debt-to-equity ratio
- Debt service coverage ratio
- Interest coverage ratio
- Burn rate and runway at current burn

## 🟪 Integrity

> Ethical and governance domains that ensure consistent values-based decision-making, transparency, and sustainable stakeholder trust.

### `I1` · Transparency

*Integrity × Availability*

> Clarity and visibility of decision-making and action rationale

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Decision-making process documentation
- Information accessibility and disclosure
- Disclosure compliance rate
- Communication clarity and openness

### `I2` · Alignment

*Integrity × Adaptability*

> Coherence between expressed values and evolving actions

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Values-to-action consistency
- Values violation rate and response
- Principle adherence during change
- Ethical decision-making under pressure

### `I3` · Accountability

*Integrity × Effectiveness*

> Ability to identify responsible actors and ensure answerability

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Responsibility assignment clarity
- Incident tracking and ownership
- Issue resolution time and closure rate
- Performance accountability systems

### `I4` · Rigor

*Integrity × Efficiency*

> Lean integration of principled behavior into processes and decisions

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Ethics embedded in workflows
- Compliance process efficiency
- Audit findings and remediation rate
- Policy enforcement without bureaucracy

### `I5` · Trust

*Integrity × Sustainability*

> Durable confidence from stakeholders that principles will hold over time

**Examples of what it covers:**

- Stakeholder trust scores
- Reputation stability over time
- Ethical track record consistency
- Regulatory compliance and violation history
